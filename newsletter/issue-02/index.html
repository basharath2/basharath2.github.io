An AI management system in build order, and when the provider’s backups go too

A worked example

An AI management system in build order

The free AIMS Starter Kit for ISO/IEC 42001:2023 went up on 7 November; a 12-minute explainer followed on 10 November. The order it teaches: find the AI first (an inventory behind the scope), then policy and roles, one method for AI risk and for impact on people, the Statement of Applicability after the risk work, and evidence an auditor can test.

The test we suggest: pick one AI system and follow it from the inventory to its evidence. Where the thread breaks is where the audit finding will be.

Do this week: list five AI systems you know about, then ask three teams which ones they use.

An incident worth learning from

A provider’s cloud and some of its backups, wiped

On 2 November a large hosting provider was hit by an attack on its management servers and data was erased. By 6 November it said its cloud had been destroyed and that file backups would exist for only about half of its virtual server users. Its restoration figures are its own, unaudited and server-level, and the way in is still undisclosed.

The useful part for the rest of us is not the attack. It is that some of the backups were lost in the same incident as production. The question for your own setup is the same one: can whatever reaches production also reach the recovery copy? A car-sharing service in the same incident was running again the same afternoon because it had another contractor to move to.

Three questions for every critical provider:

  • Can production’s administrators delete our recovery copy? In the kits: ISMS Statement of Applicability (05) and BCMS IT Disaster Recovery Runbook (06).
  • What is the longest outage this provider could cause, and can the activity survive it? In the kits: BCMS Business Impact Analysis Workbook (02) and Strategy and Solutions Register (04), where the “meets RTO/RPO” flag shows the gap.
  • Where is our exit? Domain control, DNS, a data copy, a second provider, time to switch. In the kits: BCMS 04, ISMS 04 and 05, SMS Scope Statement (01) and the Service Catalogue tab of the SMS Workbook (04). None of them is an exit plan; add one line per provider.
