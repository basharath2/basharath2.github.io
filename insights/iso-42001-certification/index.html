ISO/IEC 42001 certification: what it is, how it works, and how to get ready

What ISO/IEC 42001 certification is, how the audit path works, what drives cost and time, and a free readiness check. Plain English, no sign-up.

What ISO/IEC 42001 certification is and what it covers

ISO/IEC 42001 certification is formal confirmation by a certification body, ideally one that is accredited, that your AI management system (AIMS) conforms to ISO/IEC 42001:2023. It is voluntary, issued by a certification body (not by ISO), and runs on a three-year cycle. It certifies how you govern AI, not the safety of any single model.

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (first edition, published December 2023) is the first international management system standard for artificial intelligence. It applies to any organisation that develops, provides or uses AI systems, whatever its size or sector.

An AIMS is the set of policies, roles, risk processes, controls and records that keep AI use accountable. The certificate covers only the scope you define (business units, AI systems and life-cycle roles), so read the scope statement on any certificate you rely on.

What the standard asks for

The standard asks you to run AI governance as a management system: decide what is in scope, set direction from the top, assess and treat AI risks and impacts, operate controls, check they work, and keep improving. The requirements sit in clauses 4 to 10, with a control set in Annex A.

In our own words, by clause area:

Clause 4: understand the organisation, the people affected by its AI, and its role in the AI life cycle; then fix the boundaries of the AIMS.

Clause 5: top management owns the system. It sets an AI policy, assigns responsibilities and shows visible commitment.

Clause 6: plan how risks are found and handled. This includes an AI risk assessment, a risk treatment plan, a statement of which Annex A controls apply and why, an assessment of how AI systems may affect individuals and society, and measurable objectives.

Clause 7: provide what the system needs to run: people, skills, awareness, communication and controlled documentation.

Clause 8: carry out the plans in day-to-day operation, repeat risk and impact assessments when things change, and control externally supplied AI.

Clause 9: monitor and measure, run internal audits, and hold management reviews.

Clause 10: fix nonconformities, address their causes and improve the system over time.

Annex A contains 38 controls. You decide which apply to your scope and justify every inclusion and exclusion.

Annex B gives implementation guidance for those controls. Brivellis refers to controls by number only; the control wording belongs to the standard itself.

Related guidance: ISO/IEC 42005:2025 (AI system impact assessment) and ISO/IEC 27090:2026 (AI security threats). Neither is certifiable.

The certification path, step by step

Certification follows the same route as other ISO management system standards: define scope, close gaps, run the system, check it internally, then pass a two-stage external audit and keep the certificate through yearly surveillance.

What is required, and by whom: scope, internal audit and management review are requirements of ISO/IEC 42001. The readiness check is recommended practice. Stage 1, stage 2 and surveillance are rules certification bodies follow (ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 42006), not requirements on your organisation.

1. Set the scope. Decide which business units, locations, AI systems and life-cycle roles are in.

2. Check readiness (recommended practice). Compare what you have today against clauses 4 to 10 and the Annex A controls. Record each gap with an owner and a date.

3. Build the AIMS. Write the AI policy, assign roles, run the AI risk assessment and impact assessments, prepare the statement of applicable controls, and put the controls into practice. Keep records; auditors want evidence the system operates, not just documents.

4. Run an internal audit and a management review. Audit the whole system at least once before the external audit, fix what it finds, and have top management review the results.

5. Stage 1 audit. The certification body reviews your documentation, scope and readiness.

6. Stage 2 audit. The certification body checks that the system is implemented and effective in practice. Major nonconformities must be corrected and verified before the certification decision; minor ones need an accepted corrective-action plan. That decision is made by people at the certification body who were not on the audit team.

7. Surveillance audits. At least once a year in years one and two, a shorter audit samples parts of the system and follows up earlier findings.

8. Recertification. Before the three-year certificate expires, a recertification audit reviews the whole system. A positive result starts a new three-year cycle.

Choosing an accredited certification body

Choose a certification body that is accredited specifically for ISO/IEC 42001 by a recognised national accreditation body. Accreditation is what makes a certificate independently checkable and internationally recognised.

How to check:

Ask for the accreditation certificate and its scope. It should name ISO/IEC 42001 explicitly. Accreditation for ISO/IEC 27001 or ISO 9001 alone does not extend to an AIMS.

Verify it with the accreditation body. National accreditation bodies publish directories of the certification bodies they accredit. Check the entry yourself rather than relying on a logo.

Check international recognition. Accreditation bodies that sign the international multilateral recognition arrangement have their accreditations recognised by other signatories. This arrangement was long run by the International Accreditation Forum (the IAF MLA). Since 1 January 2026 it has been run by Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated, the body that combined the IAF and ILAC, and existing recognition has carried over.

Ask about ISO/IEC 42006. ISO/IEC 42006:2025 sets extra requirements for bodies that audit and certify AI management systems, on top of the general rules in ISO/IEC 17021-1. These cover areas such as auditor competence in AI and how audit time is set. Ask how the body meets it.

Keep audit and advice separate. An accredited body must not also help build the system it certifies.

Brivellis does not recommend or endorse any certification body.

Cost drivers and timeline

Cost and duration depend mainly on how much is in scope and how much governance you already have. There is no standard price or fixed timeline; each certification body works out audit days for your specific scope.

What moves the effort:

Scope size: headcount, number of sites and business units in scope.

Number and criticality of AI systems: high-impact uses, such as decisions about people, need deeper risk and impact assessments and more audit sampling.

Your role in the AI life cycle: developing models usually involves more controls than using a third-party AI service.

Existing management systems: an operating ISO/IEC 27001 system already gives you policy control, internal audit, management review and corrective action, which can be extended rather than rebuilt.

Audit days: set by the certification body from your scope, risk and complexity, within the rules its accreditation requires. More days mean higher external audit cost.

Internal effort: time from AI, data, legal, risk and security owners.

Allow time for the system to operate before stage 2: records of risk reviews, an internal audit and a management review take months, not days.

ISO/IEC 42001 compared

ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable management system for AI governance. The frameworks below do different jobs and often sit alongside it.

For professionals: lead implementer and lead auditor routes

There are two common professional routes. A lead implementer course teaches you to build and run an AIMS. A lead auditor course teaches you to audit one, using management system audit practice.

Lead implementer: for people who will build or own the AIMS. Expect scoping, risk and impact assessment, control selection and certification preparation.

Lead auditor: for internal auditors and those aiming to audit for a certification body. Expect audit planning, evidence, sampling and reporting, based on ISO 19011 and the rules in ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 42006.

When comparing courses, check whether the personnel certificate is issued under an accredited scheme (for example, one accredited to ISO/IEC 17024), how long it stays valid, and what it needs to stay current. Auditing for a certification body usually also requires logged audit experience, not just an exam.

Where to get the standard

Buy ISO/IEC 42001:2023 from ISO directly or from your national standards body. Brivellis never hosts, reproduces or summarises the text of the standard.

You need your own licensed copy to implement or audit against it. National standards bodies may sell it as a national adoption with their own cover. As of 21 October 2026, ISO listed edition 1 with no amendment.

FAQ

Is ISO 42001 certification mandatory?

No. ISO/IEC 42001 is a voluntary international standard, and no law requires certification against it. Organisations usually pursue it because customers, partners or procurement teams ask for evidence that AI is governed. Some contracts and tenders may name it as a requirement, which makes it necessary for that deal, not by law.

How long does ISO 42001 certification take?

There is no fixed timeline. The time depends on scope, how many AI systems are included, and how much governance already exists. Teams with a working ISO/IEC 27001 system often move faster because the management system structure is shared. Once certified, the certificate runs on a three-year cycle with surveillance audits at least yearly.

Does ISO 42001 certification mean we comply with the EU AI Act?

No. Certification shows that an organisation runs an audited AI management system, which can support AI Act work such as risk management, documentation and oversight. The Act sets its own legal obligations, roles and conformity routes, and those must be met separately. Treat 42001 as a useful foundation, not as proof of legal compliance.

Do we need ISO 27001 before ISO 42001?

No. ISO/IEC 42001 can be implemented and certified on its own. Having ISO/IEC 27001 in place helps, because both share the same management system structure, so policies, internal audit, management review and document control can be reused. Organisations can run the two as one integrated system and have them audited together.

Who can certify an organisation to ISO 42001?

An accredited certification body. Check that it is accredited for ISO/IEC 42001 by a national accreditation body that belongs to the international recognition arrangement (formerly the IAF MLA). Certificates from unaccredited bodies have no independent recognition under the international arrangement. ISO itself does not certify organisations, and Brivellis does not either.
