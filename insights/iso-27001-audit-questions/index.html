ISO/IEC 27001 audit questions: ten things auditors ask your team, and answers that hold up

The ten ISO 27001 audit questions auditors ask most: what each tests, the weak answer to avoid, the answer that holds up and the record to show.

An ISO/IEC 27001 audit is mostly a conversation. The auditor reads your documents before the visit, then spends most of the time asking people to explain something and show the record behind it. The questions are predictable. What catches teams out is rarely a hard question. It is an answer that describes what should happen instead of pointing to what did.

Below are ten ISO/IEC 27001 audit questions that come up in most ISO/IEC 27001:2022 audits, internal or certification. For each one: what it tests, the weak answer, and an answer that holds up. Requirements are paraphrased and cited by clause number; the answers are common practice, not wording from the standard. The same questions turn up among ISO 27001 interview questions for audit and GRC roles, so they work as preparation for those too.

1. “What is in scope, and why is that the boundary?”

Clause 4.3 asks you to set the boundaries of the ISMS and to look at where your activities connect with, or rely on, what sits outside it. The weak answer is "the whole company", or a department name. The answer that holds up names the services, locations, systems and teams inside the scope, names what is outside and why, and points to the interfaces (shared platforms, suppliers, the parent company's IT) and how each is managed.

2. “Show me how you found this risk, and who owns it”

Clause 6.1.2 requires a risk assessment process that gives the same kind of result each time it is run, with an owner for each risk. A register with no method behind it fails here. Show the method first (criteria and scales), then let the auditor pick any row: the asset or process, the threat, the score, the owner and the date it was last assessed.

3. “Why did you exclude this control?”

Clause 6.1.3 asks for a Statement of Applicability that justifies each control you include and each Annex A control you leave out. "It doesn't apply to us" is not a justification. Show the line in the Statement of Applicability, the reason (no risk in the register needs it, no legal or contractual requirement), and when it will be looked at again.

4. “How do you know this control works?”

Clause 9.1 asks you to decide what to monitor and measure, how, when, and who looks at the results. "We have a policy" answers a different question. Name the measure, for example the share of leavers whose access was removed within one working day, then show the latest result and who reviewed it.

5. “Which objectives did you set, and are you meeting them?”

Clause 6.2 asks for information security objectives that are measurable where that is realistic, monitored and planned. Objectives that read like slogans fail. Each one needs an owner, a target, a measure, a date and a current status you can show.

6. “What do people here need to know, and how do you check?”

Clause 7.3 is about what people must know: the policy, how they contribute, and what follows if they do not keep to it. Auditors often test this by asking someone who is not on the ISMS team. "Everyone did the training" is the weak answer. The strong one is a training record plus a person who can say, in their own words, how they would report an incident.

7. “What changed since the last audit, and how did you handle it?”

Clause 6.3, added in the 2022 edition, asks that you plan a change to the ISMS before you make it. "Nothing changed" is almost never true: a new supplier, a new cloud service, a reorganisation. Show the change record and the risk register and Statement of Applicability lines it updated.

8. “Show me your last internal audit. Who audited what?”

Clause 9.2 requires an audit programme, with criteria and scope for each audit, run by people who have no stake in the work they check. A process owner auditing their own process is the classic finding. Show the programme, the last report, its findings and the follow-up, and the auditor's independence from the area audited.

9. “When did top management last review the ISMS, and what did they decide?”

Clause 9.3 asks top management to review the ISMS on a set schedule. Minutes that only "noted" a report are the weak answer. Show the inputs (audit results, objectives, incidents, changes) and the decisions and actions that came out of the meeting, each with an owner and a date.

10. “Tell me about something that went wrong”

Clause 10.2 deals with what you do when something does not meet a requirement: react, find the cause, act, and check the action worked. "Nothing has gone wrong" worries an auditor more than a record with findings in it. Show one nonconformity or incident with its cause, the action taken and the effectiveness check.

How to prepare: follow one thread

Pick one risk and walk it end to end: register row, Statement of Applicability line, the control, the evidence, the measure, the management review that saw it. If a link is missing, that is where the finding will be. Then rehearse with three people who are not on the ISMS team, and practise one habit: answer with "let me show you", never with "we always".

Where AI helps, and where it must not

An AI assistant can turn your Statement of Applicability and evidence register into practice questions for a rehearsal. It must not write answers you then present as fact, or create records that never existed. Every output is a draft for a person to check.
