Business Resilience

Keep critical services running through disruption: business continuity under ISO 22301, business impact analysis, disaster recovery and crisis management.

What business resilience is

Business resilience is the ability to keep critical services running through disruption and to recover them within limits the business has agreed in advance.

ISO 22301 is the international standard for a business continuity management system (BCMS). Disaster recovery, which restores technology, is one part of it, not the whole.

How it is structured

ISO 22301:2019 uses the shared management system clauses. Its operational core is clause 8. In our own words:

  • Clauses 4 to 7: context, scope, leadership, the continuity policy, objectives and the support the system needs.
  • Clause 8.2: business impact analysis and risk assessment, which set priorities and recovery targets.
  • Clause 8.3: continuity strategies and solutions that can meet those targets.
  • Clause 8.4: plans and procedures, including the response structure, warning and communication.
  • Clauses 8.5 and 8.6: an exercise programme, then evaluation of the plans and capabilities.
  • Clauses 9 and 10: monitoring, internal audit, management review and improvement.

The business impact analysis produces three numbers: the maximum tolerable period of disruption (MTPD), the recovery time objective (RTO) and the recovery point objective (RPO). The same numbers should run from the analysis to the plan and the exercise report.

Who it’s for

Business continuity, IT operations and risk teams.

Common mistakes

What reviews and audits most often find. This is common practice, not the standard's text.

IT sets the recovery targets. The RTO is taken from how long the last restore took. That is a fact about the technology, not a decision about the business.

Numbers that drift. The BIA says four hours, the plan says eight and the exercise report gives no figure, so nobody can show the target was tested.

Plans that cover systems only. Recovery steps exist for servers, but not for people, suppliers, facilities, manual workarounds or the decisions needed under pressure.

Exercises that confirm a document. The scenario checks that the plan was followed, not whether the organisation could coordinate, decide and communicate.

Questions

What is the difference between RTO and RPO?

The RTO is the target time to resume a service after disruption. The RPO is the point in time to which data must be restored. Both are set in the business impact analysis, and the RTO is set within the MTPD (8.2.3).

Is disaster recovery the same as business continuity?

No. Disaster recovery restores technology. Business continuity keeps the service running, which also needs people, suppliers, facilities and workarounds.

Who should set recovery targets?

The business owners of each service, through the business impact analysis. IT says what is achievable and at what cost, and the gap is a decision for management.

How often should we exercise?

Clause 8.5 of ISO 22301:2019 asks for exercises at planned intervals and when significant changes occur. Common practice is at least one exercise a year for each critical service.
