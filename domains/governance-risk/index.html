Governance & Risk

How organisations decide, prioritise and stay in control: integrated management systems, ISO 31000 risk management, internal audit, enterprise architecture and digital transformation.

What governance and risk cover

Governance and risk is how an organisation sets direction, takes risk on purpose and checks that it stays in control as technology changes.

It covers risk management under ISO 31000, integrated management systems that serve several standards at once, internal audit, and AI governance under ISO/IEC 42001.

How it is structured

Three standards shape most of the work. In our own words:

  • ISO 31000:2018 gives guidance, not certifiable requirements: principles (clause 4), a framework (clause 5) and a process (clause 6) to set criteria, assess, treat, monitor, record and report risk.
  • ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is a certifiable management system standard for AI. Beside the shared clauses it sets out an AI risk assessment (6.1.2), risk treatment (6.1.3) and an AI system impact assessment (6.1.4).
  • ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A lists 38 controls; a Statement of Applicability records which apply and why.
  • An integrated management system uses the shared clause structure so one policy set, one risk method, one audit programme and one management review serve several standards.

Common mistakes

What reviews and audits most often find. This is common practice, not the standard's text.

No list of the AI in use. Scope is drawn before anyone has found the AI features inside existing tools, so the boundary leaves systems out.

A different risk scale for every standard. Security, continuity, service and AI risks are scored on separate scales and registers, so management cannot compare them.

Banning AI instead of governing it. A ban pushes use out of sight. An acceptable-use policy and an inventory bring it back where it can be managed.

Exclusions without reasons. Controls are marked not applicable with no justification an auditor can test.

Questions

Is there a certificate for ISO 31000?

No. ISO 31000 is guidance. Organisations use it to design one risk method, then apply that method inside certifiable systems such as ISO/IEC 27001 or ISO/IEC 42001.

Is ISO/IEC 42001 certifiable?

Yes, by an accredited certification body. Brivellis does not certify organisations; our material is education and templates, not certification or audit advice.

How is an AI impact assessment different from an AI risk assessment?

Risk asks what could go wrong for the organisation. Impact asks who outside it could be affected, and how. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 treats them in clauses 6.1.2 and 6.1.4.

What is an integrated management system?

One set of shared processes (policy, risk, documents, internal audit, management review) that serves several management system standards at once instead of running each separately.
