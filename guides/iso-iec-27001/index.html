ISO/IEC 27001: what it is, how certification works, and where to start

A plain-language guide to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 with Amendment 1:2024: what the standard covers, how certification works, what people often get wrong, and the free Brivellis templates and course that follow it.

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for an information security management system (ISMS): the decisions, documents and routines an organisation uses to understand its information security risks, treat them, and show that the treatment works. It is the main requirements standard of the ISO/IEC 27000 family, the one an organisation is certified against for information security; most of the rest of the family is guidance that supports it.

The current edition is ISO/IEC 27001:2022, amended in February 2024 (Amendment 1:2024) to add climate change to the issues an organisation considers when it sets up its ISMS. There is no 2026 edition. Certificates issued against the 2013 edition had to move to the 2022 edition by 31 October 2025.

What it is, and what it is not

It is a management system standard. It describes a way of running information security, with leadership, risk assessment, controls chosen for reasons, evidence, internal audit and management review. It does not prescribe a technology or a product.

It is risk-based. The controls you apply follow from your own risk assessment. Two organisations can both conform and have quite different control sets.

It is not a checklist of 93 controls. Annex A lists 93 controls as a reference you compare against. You decide which apply, record why in a Statement of Applicability, and may add controls of your own.

It is not law. ISO standards are voluntary. The pressure to conform usually comes from customers, contracts and tenders, or from a regulator that chooses to refer to the standard. There are no fines for not conforming to ISO/IEC 27001 itself.

Who it applies to

Any organisation that holds information worth protecting, of any size and in any sector. In practice the push comes most often from service providers and their customers: managed IT and cloud providers, software companies, outsourcers, and any supplier that handles a customer's data. The scope can be the whole organisation or a defined part, such as one service, site or business unit, as long as the boundary is stated and its interfaces are understood.

How the standard is built

Like other ISO management system standards, ISO/IEC 27001 follows a common high-level structure. Clauses 1 to 3 set out scope, references and terms; the requirements sit in clauses 4 to 10. In our own words:

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 4 to 10, summarised in our own words

ClauseWhat it asks for, in short
4Understand the organisation, its internal and external issues (including climate since Amendment 1:2024) and the needs of interested parties; set the ISMS scope and its processes
5Top management direction: an information security policy, roles and responsibilities, and visible commitment
6Plan the risk work (a defined method, a risk assessment and a risk treatment plan with a Statement of Applicability), measurable objectives, and changes made in a planned way
7Give the ISMS the people, money and skills it needs, make sure staff know their part, decide what is communicated to whom, and keep its documents and records controlled
8Run what was planned: carry out risk assessments and treatment at intervals and on change, and control what is outsourced
9Monitor and measure, run internal audits, and hold management reviews
10Act on nonconformities, fix their causes, and keep improving

Annex A lists 93 controls in four themes: organisational (5.1 to 5.37), people (6.1 to 6.8), physical (7.1 to 7.14) and technological (8.1 to 8.34). ISO/IEC 27002:2022 is the companion guidance on implementing them; it is not certifiable.

How certification works

  1. Build and run the ISMS for a period: scope, policy, risk assessment and treatment, the Statement of Applicability, controls in operation, evidence, at least one internal audit and one management review.
  2. Choose a certification body that is accredited for ISO/IEC 27001 by an accreditation body that takes part in the international mutual recognition arrangements for accreditation. That is what makes the certificate widely recognised.
  3. Stage 1 audit: the auditor reviews your documented ISMS and readiness; in practice this is often done remotely.
  4. Stage 2 audit: the auditor checks that the ISMS works in practice, by sampling evidence, interviewing people and following risks through to controls.
  5. Certificate issued for three years, with surveillance audits in the years between and a recertification audit before it expires.

Two points are often mixed up. Certification bodies certify organisations; accreditation bodies accredit certification bodies. And organisations are certified to ISO/IEC 27001, while people take separate personal qualifications, such as lead implementer or lead auditor courses.

How it relates to other frameworks

ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 (service management): the same high-level structure, so policy, document control, internal audit and management review can serve both. Security of the services sits in ISO/IEC 27001; how the services are run sits in ISO/IEC 20000-1.

ISO 22301:2019 (business continuity): ISO/IEC 27001 asks that security holds up when things go wrong and that technology is ready to meet continuity targets (Annex A 5.29 and 5.30); ISO 22301 covers continuity of the whole organisation.

NIST CSF 2.0: a voluntary framework of outcomes, not a certifiable standard. Many organisations use ISO/IEC 27001 as the management system and the CSF as a way to report on outcomes. The free ISMS kit includes a crosswalk between the two (a Brivellis mapping, not an official equivalence).

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI management) and ISO/IEC 27090:2026 (AI security guidance): 42001 is a separate certifiable standard for governing AI; 27090 is guidance on AI-specific threats. Organisations that use AI can run all three as layers of one system.

Common misreadings

“We need all 93 controls.” No. You need a reasoned decision for each one, in the Statement of Applicability.

“Our cloud provider is certified, so we are too.” A provider's certificate covers the provider's scope. Your own ISMS still has to manage your use of the service.

“Certification means we are secure.” It means an auditor from an accredited certification body found a working management system for the stated scope at the time of the audit. It is evidence of discipline, not a guarantee.

“The policy is the ISMS.” The policy is one document. Auditors look for the risk work, the decisions that follow from it, and evidence that the controls operate.

“Our people can be ISO/IEC 27001 certified.” Organisations are certified; people hold personal qualifications from training and exam providers.

Frequently asked questions

Is ISO/IEC 27001 mandatory?

No. It is a voluntary international standard. It becomes a requirement only when a contract, a tender or a regulator asks for it.

Which edition should we use?

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 with Amendment 1:2024. There is no newer edition as of November 2026, and the transition from the 2013 edition ended on 31 October 2025.

How long does certification take?

It depends on scope and starting point. Many small and mid-sized organisations take several months to build and run the ISMS before a certification audit, because the auditor expects to see it operating, with evidence, an internal audit and a management review.

What is the Statement of Applicability?

The record of every Annex A control: whether it applies, why, and whether it is implemented, plus any controls you added from elsewhere. It links the risk treatment to the controls and is one of the documents auditors read most closely.

Do we need software to run an ISMS?

No. A small organisation can run an ISMS with well-kept documents and spreadsheets. What matters is that the risk, control and evidence records are current and consistent.

What does the 2024 amendment change?

It adds climate change to the issues an organisation considers when it sets up its ISMS, and to what interested parties may require. Decide whether climate change is relevant to your ISMS, and record why.
