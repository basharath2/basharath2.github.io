ISO/IEC 20000-1: what it is, how certification works, and where to start

A plain-language guide to ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 with Amendment 1:2024: what the standard covers, how certification works, what people often get wrong, and the free Brivellis templates and course that follow it.

ISO/IEC 20000-1 is the international standard for a service management system (SMS): the way an organisation plans, agrees, delivers, supports and improves the services it provides, and shows that it does so under control. It is the requirements part of the ISO/IEC 20000 series, the one an organisation is certified against; most other parts are guidance or supporting documents, for example ISO/IEC 20000-2:2019 on applying the requirements and ISO/IEC 20000-3:2019 on defining the scope.

The current edition is ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018, amended in February 2024 (Amendment 1:2024) so that an organisation determines whether climate change is a relevant issue (4.1) and recognises that interested parties can have climate-related requirements (4.2). ISO last reviewed and confirmed the 2018 edition in 2023, and there is no newer edition. The transition from the 2011 edition ended in March 2022, after a six-month extension during the COVID-19 pandemic; certificates to that edition are no longer valid.

What it is, and what it is not

It is a management system standard. It describes a way of running services under control, with leadership, planning, agreed service levels, defined processes, evidence, internal audit and management review. It does not prescribe a tool, a product or an operating model.

It is about services, not departments. The scope names the services you provide and to whom; the teams and sites that deliver them follow from that. Suppliers and other parties that contribute to those services sit outside the SMS, but you stay accountable and must show you control their part. Two organisations with the same structure can have quite different scopes.

It is not a process library to copy. Clause 8 sets the outcomes for a set of service management activities. How you design each process, and how much you write down beyond what the standard specifies, is your decision, as long as you can show it working.

It is not law. ISO standards are voluntary. The pressure to conform usually comes from customers, contracts and tenders. There are no fines for not conforming to ISO/IEC 20000-1 itself.

Who it applies to

Any organisation that provides services to customers, inside or outside the organisation, of any size. In practice the push comes most often from managed service and cloud providers, outsourcers, shared service centres and internal technology functions whose customers or tenders ask for evidence of controlled service delivery. You choose which services are in scope, the whole portfolio or a defined set, and state the boundary. To claim conformity, every requirement in clauses 4 to 10 applies, and you must show control over what other parties contribute; if other parties provide or operate all the services, service components or processes in scope, conformity cannot be claimed (1.2). Accredited certification is offered mainly for IT and technology-enabled services, so check with your certification body if yours are different.

How the standard is built

Like other ISO management system standards, ISO/IEC 20000-1 follows a common high-level structure. Clauses 1 to 3 set out scope, references and terms; the requirements sit in clauses 4 to 10. In our own words:

ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 clauses 4 to 10, summarised in our own words

ClauseWhat it asks for, in short
4Understand the organisation, its internal and external issues (including climate since Amendment 1:2024) and the needs of interested parties; set the SMS scope in terms of services, and establish the SMS
5Top management direction: a service management policy, roles and responsibilities, and visible commitment
6Plan for risks and opportunities, set measurable service management objectives, and write a plan for the SMS itself
7Give the SMS the people, money, skills and knowledge it needs, make sure staff know their part, decide what is communicated to whom, and keep its documents and records controlled
8Run the services: plan and control operations, manage the set of services and the parties that deliver them, agreements with customers and suppliers, budgets, demand and capacity, changes and releases, incidents and requests, and keep the services available, continuous and secure (see below)
9Monitor and measure, run internal audits, hold management reviews, and report on how the services perform
10Act on nonconformities, fix their causes, and keep improving

Clause 8 is where ISO/IEC 20000-1 differs most from other management system standards. In short: 8.1 covers planning and controlling operations; 8.2 the services themselves, including planning them, keeping other parties involved under control, the service catalogue, assets and configuration information; 8.3 relationships and agreements with customers and suppliers, including service levels; 8.4 budgeting, demand and capacity; 8.5 changes, how new or changed services are designed and brought into live use, and releases; 8.6 incidents, service requests and problems; 8.7 the availability, continuity and information security of the services. There is no controls annex like Annex A of ISO/IEC 27001.

How certification works

  1. Build and run the SMS for a period: scope, policy, SMS plan, agreed service levels, the clause 8 activities in operation, service reports, evidence, at least one internal audit and one management review.
  2. Choose a certification body that is accredited for ISO/IEC 20000-1 by an accreditation body that takes part in the international mutual recognition arrangements for accreditation. That is what makes the certificate widely recognised.
  3. Stage 1 audit: the auditor reviews your documented SMS and readiness; the certification body decides whether it is done on site or remotely.
  4. Stage 2 audit: the auditor checks that the SMS works in practice, by sampling records, interviewing people and following a service from agreement to delivery and reporting.
  5. Certification decision: you correct any nonconformities the auditor raised, then the certification body decides and issues a certificate for three years, with surveillance audits in the years between and a recertification audit before it expires.

Two points are often mixed up. Certification bodies certify organisations; accreditation bodies accredit certification bodies. And organisations are certified to ISO/IEC 20000-1, while people take separate personal qualifications, such as lead implementer or lead auditor courses.

How it relates to other frameworks

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (information security): the same high-level structure, so policy, document control, internal audit and management review can serve both. ISO/IEC 20000-1 covers the information security of the services at a summary level (8.7.3); ISO/IEC 27001 is the full management system for it.

ISO 22301:2019 (business continuity): ISO/IEC 20000-1 asks for availability and continuity to be planned for the services in scope (8.7.1 and 8.7.2); ISO 22301 covers continuity across its own scope, which can be the whole organisation, and gives those service plans the business priorities to work from.

ISO 9001:2015 (quality): the same high-level structure again. An organisation that is already certified to ISO 9001:2015 can reuse much of its management layer and add the service-specific operation in clause 8.

Common misreadings

“Our scope is the IT department.” Scope is a set of services and the customers they serve. Departments, sites and suppliers follow from the services, not the other way round.

“Outsourced work is outside the SMS.” Other parties that help deliver the services stay inside the boundary. The organisation keeps accountability and shows that it controls them (8.2.3).

“We need a service management tool first.” A tool helps with volume, but auditors look for agreed requirements, working processes and records. Many small providers start with well-kept documents and spreadsheets.

“The SLA is the SMS.” A service level agreement is one agreement. The SMS is the policy, plan, processes, reports, audits and reviews that keep every agreement met.

“Our people can be ISO/IEC 20000-1 certified.” Organisations are certified; people hold personal qualifications from training and exam providers.

Frequently asked questions

Is ISO/IEC 20000-1 mandatory?

No. It is a voluntary international standard. It becomes a requirement only when a contract, a tender or a regulator asks for it.

Which edition should we use?

ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 with Amendment 1:2024. ISO confirmed the 2018 edition in 2023, there is no newer edition as of November 2026, and the transition from the 2011 edition ended in March 2022.

Can an internal IT function be certified?

Yes. The customers of the services can be inside the organisation. The scope is then the services that function provides, with the boundary and the parties involved in delivering them made clear.

How long does certification take?

It depends on scope and starting point. Many small and mid-sized providers take several months to build and run the SMS before a certification audit, because the auditor expects to see it operating, with service reports, an internal audit and a management review.

Do we need a separate document for every process?

No. Clause 7.5.4 lists the documented information an SMS includes, but it does not ask for one document per process. Many organisations combine related processes in one procedure and keep the records that show each one working.

What does the 2024 amendment change?

It adds climate change to the issues an organisation considers when it sets up its SMS, and to what interested parties may require. Decide whether climate change is relevant to your SMS, and record why.
