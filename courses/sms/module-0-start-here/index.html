Start with a question. Has anyone ever asked you to "get us ISO 20000 certified"? Here's what usually happens next. You open the standard. And three weeks later... there's still no scope statement. Just a blank page. That blank page is where most projects stall. This course fixes it. What will you walk away with? Over eight short modules, we'll build the documented core of a service management system, an SMS, against ISO/IEC 20000-1. You'll leave with a readiness score, a scope statement, a policy with measurable objectives, a catalogue and SLA, an SMS plan, a KPI register, an internal audit programme and a management review pack.

Every lesson follows three steps, so remember them. Step one: we open a template from the free SMS Starter Kit. Step two: we study a worked example for Falcon Ridge IT Services. They're fictional, but their problems are very real. Step three: your turn, a short exercise on your own organisation. Template, example, your turn. Each takes three to six minutes, about one coffee. Who's this for? Service managers, ITSM process owners, governance and quality leads, and MSP or cloud teams heading for certification.

The course assumes you know incident, change and SLA. You don't need to have run an ISO project before. That part is what this course is for. Plan on two to four weeks. Think of it as building a house. Module 1 surveys the site: where do you stand? Modules 2 to 4 lay the foundations: scope, policy, catalogue and SLA. Modules 5 and 6 raise the structure: planning, measurement, audit and review. And Module 7? The finishing: tailoring it all with AI, safely.

Three ground rules. One: get your own official copy of ISO/IEC 20000-1, from the ISO Store, the IEC Webstore or your national standards body. The links are below. We cite clause numbers, never the standard's text, so keep it open. Two: this isn't a certification, and it isn't audit or legal advice. Certification comes from an accredited certification body. Three: before you rely on any template you've tailored, have someone competent review it. One question before the next lesson.

If an auditor walked in tomorrow, what's the first document they'd ask to see? Hold that thought. Next up: the standard in five minutes, and what auditors actually look for. See you there.

ISO/IEC 20000-1 in five minutes: structure, the SMS, what auditors look for

Transcript

So, the auditor walks in. What do they ask for first? Let's find out, starting with what they audit against: ISO/IEC 20000-1, the international standard for service management systems. In five minutes: what it is, how it's organised, what auditors want. The series has several parts, but three matter. Part one sets the requirements, the only one you're certified against. Part two is guidance on applying part one. Part ten covers concepts and vocabulary.

We use part one, 2018 edition, with its 2024 amendment. That amendment requires you to decide whether climate change is relevant to your SMS, and notes that interested parties can have climate-related requirements. More in Module 2. In 2025, ISO published part sixteen, guidance on sustainability within an SMS. In 2026, work began on part nineteen, guidance on using AI in an SMS. A common question: isn't this just ITIL®? No. ITIL® is a best-practice framework: how to run service management well.

ISO 20000 sets auditable requirements: what must be in place, with evidence. Many organisations use ITIL® practices to meet them, and in Module 5 we map one to the other. Now, structure. It's shared with ISO 27001, 9001, and 22301. Requirements live in clauses 4 to 10. Clause 4: who you are and what the SMS covers, including scope in 4.3. Clause 5: top management's role: policy, roles, commitment. Clause 6: risks, objectives and the SMS plan. Clause 7: people, resources and documents.

Clause 8: running the services. Clause 9: checking it works: monitoring, internal audit, management review, service reporting. Clause 10: fixing and improving. Here's the key point. Clause 8 is the heart of the standard, and the biggest part of any audit. It groups the practices. Planning and controlling the work. What you offer: catalogue, assets, configuration. Customers and suppliers: business relationships, service levels, suppliers. Money and capacity: budgeting, demand, capacity.

Changing services: change, release, deployment. Fixing and fulfilling: incidents, requests, problems. And keeping services safe: availability, continuity, information security. Certification? An accredited certification body audits you in two stages. Stage 1 checks documentation and readiness. Stage 2 checks the system actually operates. The certificate usually lasts three years, with surveillance audits between and recertification at the end. And it states your scope.

That's why scope is usually the first document an auditor asks for, and why Module 2 matters. Auditors look for three things. The documented information required: scope, policy, plan, procedures. Evidence it runs: tickets, reports, minutes and records over a real period. And a working improvement loop: internal audits done, reviews held, nonconformities fixed. Documents alone won't certify you. Without them, there's nothing to audit, and no evidence to generate.

That's what we build: the documented core, so your SMS can start running. Where do those documents come from? Next, you'll download the kit and meet Falcon Ridge IT Services, the company we build alongside you.

Enough theory. Let's get you set up. Below this video is the SMS Starter Kit for ISO/IEC 20000-1, one zip file. Download it, unzip it into a working folder, and keep the original zip as a clean copy. What's inside? File 00, Read Me First. 01, the SMS Scope Statement. 02, the Service Management Policy. 03, the Service Level Agreement. 04, the SMS Workbook spreadsheet. 05, the AI Tailoring Prompt Pack. 06, the SMS Plan. 07, the management review and nonconformity pack.

Plus 00a, the Responsible AI Use Guide, 08, the AI-use log, and 09, the capstone rubric. A "b" after the number, like 02b, means it's completed for our worked example. Those are your model answers. The workbook does the heavy lifting. Seven tabs: Readiness Checklist, Risk Register, Internal Audit checklist, Audit Programme, Service Catalogue, KPI Register, and a Crosswalk to ITIL® and ISO 27001. Scores, risk levels, coverage and KPI status calculate automatically.

You just enter data. Open Read Me First and find the conventions. They apply everywhere. Text in square brackets is a placeholder: replace it, remove the brackets. Shaded boxes are guidance: delete them before issuing. And every document starts with a document control table. Keep it. Auditors often check it early, under clause 7.5. Now, meet Falcon Ridge IT Services. Fictional, and we'll build their SMS alongside yours. Three services in scope: Managed Cloud Workplace, Service Desk and Managed Infrastructure, for business units in Northport and Westbrook.

Obligations include local data protection law and national cybersecurity controls, like many organisations. Notice how concrete it is. Objectives aren't "improve quality". They're measurable. SLA targets met at least 95 percent of the time. Changes causing incidents, 5 percent or less. Customer satisfaction at least 4.2 out of five. Each has an owner. That's the bar for your documents. Before the next module, three things. One: save the kit in a folder you'll keep.

Two: copy every template, renamed with your organisation's prefix, like Falcon Ridge's FR. Three: write one line naming who you're building for. Your organisation, or Falcon Ridge if practising. That's Module 0 done. Take the quiz. Then, in Module 1, we open the Readiness Checklist and ask: where exactly do you stand?
