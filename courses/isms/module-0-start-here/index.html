Someone has just asked you to "get us ISO 27001". You open the standard, read it twice, and a week later there's still no risk register, no Statement of Applicability, and no clear first step. That's normal. This course takes you from that blank page to a documented, working core. Over eight short modules, we'll build the core of an information security management system, an ISMS, against ISO 27001, the 2022 edition with its 2024 amendment.

You'll leave with a context and scope document, a security policy with objectives, a risk method and a risk register, a Statement of Applicability for all ninety-three Annex A controls, an evidence register, an internal audit and management review pack, and a crosswalk to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework. Every lesson follows three steps.

First, we open a template from the free Brivellis ISMS Starter Kit. Second, we study a worked example for Falcon Ridge IT Services, a fictional IT provider with very real problems. Third, your turn: a short exercise on your own organisation. Template, example, your turn. Each lesson takes three to five minutes. Who is this for? Information security and IT managers starting an ISMS, GRC and compliance practitioners, and service providers whose customers keep asking for ISO 27001 evidence.

You should know basic terms like risk, access control and incident. You don't need to have run an ISO project before.

Here's the path. Module 1 sets context and scope. Module 2, leadership, policy and objectives. Modules 3 and 4 are the heart of the course: the risk method, the risk register and the Statement of Applicability. Module 5 turns controls into evidence. Module 6 measures, audits and reviews. And Module 7 connects to NIST, tailors everything with AI, and maps your road to certification.

Three ground rules. One: get an official copy of ISO 27001, 2022, and its 2024 amendment, from the ISO Store, the IEC Webstore or your national standards body. We cite clause and control numbers, never the standard's text, so keep yours open. Two: this course is not a certification, and not audit or legal advice. Three: before you rely on anything you've tailored, have someone competent review it.

One question before we move on. What's the first document an auditor reads in an ISMS? Hold that thought. Next: the standard in five minutes, and what auditors actually look for.

What does an auditor actually test you against? ISO 27001, the international standard for information security management systems. In five minutes: how it's built, what changed recently, and what auditors look for. We use the 2022 edition, the third, together with Amendment 1 from 2024. The amendment asks you to decide whether climate change is a relevant issue for your ISMS, and notes that interested parties can have climate-related requirements.

It's a short change, but auditors check it, so Module 1 covers it. The companion standard, ISO 27002, gives guidance on each control. It's useful, but you're certified against 27001 only. The requirements live in clauses four to ten, the same structure as other management system standards, such as ISO 9001. Clause four: context and scope. Five: leadership and policy.

Six: planning, including risk assessment, risk treatment and objectives. Seven: support, from resources and competence to documented information. Eight: operation, where the risk processes run. Nine: performance evaluation, including internal audit and management review. And ten: improvement. Then there's Annex A: ninety-three controls in four themes. Thirty-seven organisational, eight people, fourteen physical and thirty-four technological.

Here's the part people miss. Annex A isn't a checklist you must implement in full. It's a reference list you compare your risk treatment against, so nothing necessary is missed. The Statement of Applicability records that comparison, control by control.

So the engine of the standard is risk. Clauses 6.1.2 and 6.1.3 decide which controls you need. Get the risk method right, and the Statement of Applicability almost writes itself. Get it wrong, and every control decision looks arbitrary to an auditor. Certification happens in two stages. Stage 1 reviews your documents and readiness. Stage 2 tests whether the ISMS actually runs, with records over a real period.

The certificate lasts three years, with surveillance audits in between, and it states your scope, word for word. Auditors look for three things. The documented information the standard requires, such as the scope, the policy, the risk method and the Statement of Applicability. Evidence that the controls operate. And a working improvement loop: internal audits done, management reviews held, nonconformities fixed.

Let's open the toolbox. In this lesson you'll download the free ISMS Starter Kit, see how its thirteen files fit together, and meet the company we'll build alongside you. The kit comes as one zip. Inside: a Read Me, a short guide to using AI responsibly, and files 01 to 10. Documents for context and scope, the policy and objectives, the risk method and the audit pack.

Workbooks for the risk register, the Statement of Applicability, the evidence register, the audit programme, the NIST crosswalk and the AI-use log. The files form one chain, and it's worth remembering. Context feeds risk. Risk picks controls. Controls point to evidence. Evidence is sampled in the internal audit. And management review looks at all of it and decides. Each workbook has a check column that reads OK, or tells you exactly what's broken in that chain.

Now meet Falcon Ridge IT Services, the shared IT services company of a group. A head office and operations centre in Northport, a third-party colocation data centre, field support in Westbrook, and one hundred and thirty-two people. They run three services for the group's business units: a managed cloud workplace, a service desk and managed infrastructure. Falcon Ridge's example runs through 2027. Twenty risks. A Statement of Applicability with ninety-one controls applicable and two excluded.

An internal audit in June with two minor findings, and a management review in July. The example keeps honest gaps on purpose: a control with no evidence yet, an overdue action, a record past its review date. You'll watch the checks catch every one. Three conventions. Blue text in square brackets is a placeholder: replace it. Yellow boxes are guidance, and blue boxes are the Falcon Ridge example: delete both before you issue a document.

In the workbooks, yellow cells are inputs, white cells calculate, and grey cells hold the Falcon Ridge example. Never type over a formula. One more thing you'll notice. The kit lists Annex A controls by number, with a short description in our own words. It doesn't reproduce the control titles or text, which belong to ISO. Read those in your licensed copy, side by side with the kit.
