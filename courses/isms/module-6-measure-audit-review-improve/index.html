Measure, audit, review, improve

ISMS in Practice · Module 6 · 4 lessons · 9 minutes of video

Monitoring and measurement, an internal audit, a management review that produces decisions, and corrective action.

Monitoring and measurement that someone uses

Lightly edited for reading; the video is the original.

If you don't measure it, you can't review it. Clause 9.1 asks you to decide what to monitor and measure, how, when, who analyses the results, and how they're evaluated. Measure two things: how well the controls work, and how well the ISMS works. Falcon Ridge's measures come straight from its objectives and its awareness programme: multi-factor coverage, patch speed, treatment actions closed on time, awareness completion, and phishing report rates.

For each measure, write the method once: data source, formula, frequency, owner, and who analyses it. "Patch compliance" is a phrase. "Critical fixes applied within fourteen days, divided by critical findings, from the monthly scan report, analysed by the head of infrastructure" is a measure. Measuring isn't the end. Clause 9.1 asks you to evaluate the results. Falcon Ridge brings its measures to management review, and every measure that misses its target gets an owner and a decision. Three traps. Measuring what's easy instead of what matters.

Collecting numbers nobody looks at. And no baseline, so nobody can tell whether things improved. Start with five measures you'll actually use. Your turn. Pick five measures from your objectives and your riskiest controls, write the method for each, and record a baseline. Next: planning and running an internal audit.

Plan and run an ISMS internal audit

The internal audit is your rehearsal for certification, and a requirement in its own right. Clause 9.2 asks for an audit programme, audits at planned intervals, objective auditors, and results reported to management. Open the Audit Programme and NC Log workbook. The programme covers three years: every clause each year, every Annex A theme at least once in three years, and high-priority areas every year.

The check column flags any area that isn't covered. Objectivity matters. Auditors must not audit their own work. Falcon Ridge uses the group's internal audit team, who have no IT operations role. A small organisation can swap auditors between departments, or bring in an outside auditor for a few days. The audit pack, file 07, gives a checklist for clauses four to ten: twenty-six questions in our own words, each with where to look.

Add questions for your own policies, and sample controls from the Statement of Applicability: is each one in place, is its evidence current, and does it work for the risks that rely on it? Grade findings consistently. Major: a requirement not met at all, or a failure that puts the ISMS's outcomes in doubt. Minor: a partial or one-off lapse.

Observations and opportunities for improvement sit below those. Falcon Ridge's June audit found two minor nonconformities. First, control 5.7 marked implemented, with no record of threat information being collected or used. Second, storage media handling records past their review date. Both were real, both were small, and both were gaps the workbooks had already flagged. Your turn. Set up your three-year programme, choose an objective auditor, and plan your first internal audit well before your Stage 1 audit. Next: management review.

Management review that produces decisions

Management review is where top management proves its commitment. Clause 9.3 asks for reviews at planned intervals, with set inputs, and outputs that include decisions. File 07, Part C, lists the inputs, a to g, in our own wording. Actions from earlier reviews. Changes in issues, including climate. Changes in interested parties' needs. Performance: nonconformities, measurement results, audit results and objectives.

Feedback from interested parties. Where the risk assessment and treatment plan stand. And opportunities to improve. Falcon Ridge's July review shows what good inputs look like. A summer heat peak, with cooling holding and no incident. Two minor audit findings, one already closed. Objectives on track. Awareness completion at 96.7 percent. And one overdue treatment action. And the outputs: four decisions.

Re-plan the overdue action, with an interim weekly manual check. Fund a source of threat information, to close the audit finding. Combine the security and continuity audits from 2028. And offer business units a quarterly access report.

Here's a simple test for any management review record: count the decisions. A review that only "notes" and "acknowledges" is a presentation, not a review. Every output should change something, with an owner and a date. Your turn. Draft your first review agenda from the a-to-g inputs, collect the data, and decide in advance which decisions you'll need from top management. Next: fixing nonconformities properly.

Nonconformity and corrective action

Lesson 6.4 · 2:472 minutes 47 seconds

Transcript

Lightly edited for reading; the video is the original.

Clause ten is short, and auditors read it closely. When something doesn't conform, you react, find the cause, remove the cause, check that it worked, and improve the ISMS. Let's do that properly. Five steps. React: correct it now, and deal with the consequences. Find the cause: ask why, more than once. Decide on a corrective action in proportion to the effect.

Check its effectiveness at an agreed date. And update the ISMS: a document, a control or a risk. The distinction that trips people up: a correction fixes the instance. A corrective action removes the cause. Reviewing the overdue media records is a correction. Adding next-review dates and an overdue check to the evidence register, so it can't happen again, is a corrective action.

The NC Log tab records each nonconformity: its source, the clause or control, what was found, the correction, the root cause, the corrective action, the owner, the due date and the effectiveness check. Its check flags a missing cause, an overdue action, or anything closed without an effectiveness check. Falcon Ridge's two. NC-01: the cause was treating vendor security notices as threat information, with nothing recorded.

The action: two threat sources, and a monthly summary with actions. NC-02: review dates lived in owners' calendars, not in the register. NC-02 closed in July, and NC-01's action is due at the end of August. Both get an effectiveness check at the October audit. Continual improvement, clause 10.1, isn't a separate project. It's the sum of your corrective actions, management review decisions and opportunities.

Show the trend: fewer findings, faster closure, better measures. Your turn. Take one problem you already know about. Write the correction, ask why five times, and write a corrective action that removes the cause. Next, Module 7: NIST, AI, and the road to certification.
