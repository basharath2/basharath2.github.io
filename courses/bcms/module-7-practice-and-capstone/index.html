Practice: find the mistakes in an AI-drafted BIA

Transcript

Lightly edited for reading; the video is the original.

Time to practise. In the resources for this lesson there's a short workbook: a business impact analysis drafted by an AI tool for three fictional activities. It looks finished. It contains six planted mistakes. Your job is to find them before an auditor would.

Here's what to look for. A recovery time that is not below the MTPD. A recovery point above the data-loss tolerance. A target nobody set, with no source. A test result that never happened. A missing supplier, hidden behind a software-as-a-service tool. And a sign-off the tool simply typed in. Use the check-before-approval list from file ten. Trace every figure to the signed analysis, not the chat.

Look for the tags: to-confirm, assumption, and the DRAFT label, and notice when they're missing. Ask who said so for every target. And for every test result or sign-off, check that the record actually exists. Why practise this? Because AI output looks finished long before it is right. Clean formatting, confident numbers and a neat sign-off block are not evidence.

In continuity, an invented recovery time is worse than a blank one, because people will plan around it. When you've found your six, open the answer key in the same resources. For each mistake, it shows where it is, why it matters, and what the corrected entry should look like. Then do what Falcon Ridge does: log the review in your AI use log, with who checked it and what they changed.

Your turn: download the practice workbook, find all six errors, and write one line for each: where it is, and what you'd change. Then check the answer key. Next: the capstone, and what's coming for continuity.

BCMS capstone and what's next for continuity

You've seen the whole chain: context, impact, risk, strategy, plans, exercises, evidence and review. The capstone asks you to build the core of it for your own organisation, and test it once. Part A: your scope and policy, and a signed business impact analysis for at least three activities. Part B: a strategy register with every gap decided, and a plan and runbook that use the same numbers.

Part C: one tabletop exercise, with a report and corrective actions. Part D: the AI check from the practice lesson, logged. The capstone checklist in the resources tells you what good looks like. A BCMS decays without a rhythm. Every quarter, check the contact list and run a restore test. After every change to a site, supplier or role, update the plans.

And every year, refresh the BIA and the risk assessment, run the programme, the internal audit and the management review. Three things to watch. AI agents, which hold identities and access, and can stop halfway through a task, so give them least privilege and a manual fallback. The revision of ISO 22301, now at committee draft, so check for a new edition before your next certification cycle.

And concentration on a few cloud and AI providers. If you go for certification, a certification body runs a stage one audit on your documents and readiness, then a stage two audit that tests the system runs, using records over a real period. Exercises, reviews and closed actions are what stage two looks for. That decision is yours; this course isn't a certification.

Thank you for taking this course. Start Part A this week: your scope, then three activities in the business impact analysis. Questions about the templates? Post them in the course Q-and-A. And if this helped, the free courses on service management and information security follow the same pattern.
